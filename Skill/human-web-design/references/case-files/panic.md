# Case file: Panic

- **URL:** https://panic.com/
- **Direction:** Loud Product (publisher variant)
- **Physical object it evokes:** a stack of game boxes / a toy shop window
- **Analysed:** 2026-10-28

## Tokens

| Token | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fonts | Booton (light 300 and semibold 600) + Space Mono accents | Light display weight is unusual and friendly |
| Display | 80px / 104px, weight 300 for product titles | Large but airy, not heavy |
| Type sizes in use | 15 · 16 · 24 · 40 · 42 · 48 · 80 | Big steps |
| Colour | Each section takes its palette from the game/app art; brand chrome is neutral (`#1c1c1c`, white, greys) plus a vivid P3 violet | Content-owned colour |
| Radius | 10px cards · 22.5px pills · 4px small | |

## Signature moves

1. **Every section is one product, full-bleed**, using that product's own key art and logo; the company brand steps back.
2. **A playful brand mark** (scalloped badge) at the top instead of a conventional header.
3. **Mono accents** for metadata, giving a "made by programmers" undertone.

## Why it works

A publisher's site should make each title feel like itself. Letting each section adopt its product's art creates variety and rhythm automatically, while the neutral chrome keeps it one site.

## How to adapt

- For multi-product brands, let each product own its section's palette and imagery; keep type and spacing constant to hold it together.
- A light display weight at large size can feel warmer than the usual heavy bold.

## Watch-outs

- Requires strong art for every product; one weak asset breaks the rhythm.
